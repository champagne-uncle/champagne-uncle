About

A life poured into
every glass

I didn't set out to become a champagne person. It happened the way most meaningful things do — gradually, then all at once. The first time a glass of real grower champagne landed in my hand, something clicked. Not just the bubbles. The story behind them.

Today, I support Crystal Wine Group — a community built around the belief that great wine deserves great people. Whether it's sourcing, introducing, or simply sharing, the mission stays the same: bring the right bottle to the right table.

At Les Clefs de Cristal Yangjae, we've built a space where champagne doesn't need a special occasion. Come as you are, open something interesting, and let the wine do the talking.

And at Bau Vineyard, I'm doing something I never imagined — growing Chardonnay on Korean soil. The vines are young, the ambition is not. Every season teaches something new about patience and terroir.

RM Grower · Vallée de la Marne · Extra Brut

Mousse Fils
Hommage à Cuisles

Champagne · Grower & Producer · Cuisles, Vallée de la Marne

Cédric Mousse is one of the most compelling voices in grower Champagne today. His Hommage à Cuisles is a tribute to the village where his family has farmed for generations — a single-village expression built on Pinot Meunier, vinified without malolactic fermentation to preserve tension and clarity. The nose offers white flowers, ripe pear, brioche and a subtle touch of spice. On the palate, the texture is silky yet precise, with bright citrus acidity, crushed chalk minerality and a long, saline finish. Extra Brut dosage keeps the wine taut and expressive — a portrait of terroir rather than house style.

"Mousse Fils remains one of the benchmark addresses for Meunier-driven Champagne — pure, purposeful and deeply rooted in place." — Champagne Uncle
ProducerMousse Fils (Cédric Mousse)
StyleExtra Brut · No Malo
GrapePinot Meunier · Cuisles
RegionVallée de la Marne, Champagne

Grand Cru · Aÿ · Fût de Chêne

Henri Giraud
Fût de Chêne MV20

Champagne Grand Cru · Aÿ · Pinot Noir dominant

Henri Giraud's Fût de Chêne MV20 is one of Champagne's most distinctive and deliberately crafted expressions. Aged in small Argonne oak barrels — a forest that Giraud sources and selects himself — the wine undergoes full malolactic fermentation in wood, building extraordinary texture and depth. The MV (Multi-Vintage) blend draws on reserve wines from Aÿ's grand cru vineyards, anchored by Pinot Noir with contributions of Chardonnay and Meunier. The nose is complex and warming: toasted brioche, roasted hazelnut, dried apricot, and a signature cedar-oak spice. On the palate, the mousse is creamy and persistent, the acidity perfectly integrated, and the finish long with notes of salted caramel and forest floor. A wine that rewards patience — and demands attention.

"The barrel work is seamless — you taste the oak as architecture, not ornament. This is Champagne with the soul of Burgundy." — Champagne Uncle
ProducerHenri Giraud
StyleBrut · Fût de Chêne (Argonne Oak)
GrapePinot Noir dominant · Grand Cru Aÿ
RegionAÿ, Grand Cru · Montagne de Reims

Grand Cru · Blanc de Blancs · Avize

Franck Bonville
Unisson

Champagne Grand Cru · 100% Chardonnay · Côte des Blancs

92 pts Wine Advocate

A salty, authoritative nose of white orchard fruit, lemon oil, subtle spice and chalk mineral. On the palate — creamy brioche, yuzu citrus, fine bubbles and a vivid, stony finish of impressive length. Entirely from old-vine holdings in the grand cru of Avize, aged nearly five years on the lees before disgorgement. Extra Brut dosage at just 5–6.7 g/L keeps everything sharp and precise.

"Fantastic creaminess, fine brioche toast and a core of Yuzu citrus — everything I want in Chardonnay from Champagne." — K&L Wines, January 2025

Restaurant Report · July 22, 2026

Restaurant SAN

From the World's Kitchens, Back to the Taste of Korea

On July 22nd, I sat down to experience the summer menu at Restaurant SAN. At the heart of this Michelin-recognised destination is Chef Seunghyun Cho — a Korean-born cook who spent years refining his culinary language in the world's most demanding kitchens before returning home.

His résumé reads like a pilgrimage through fine dining's inner sanctum: The French Laundry in Napa Valley, Troisgros in Roanne, Piazza Duomo in Italy, and Geranium in Copenhagen. He then made his way to San Francisco, joining Chef Corey Lee at the three-Michelin-starred Benu, where he spent roughly eight years as Chef de Cuisine.

Benu is a restaurant that interprets the flavours of Korea, China, and East Asia through the precision of Western fine dining. Working there, Chef Cho didn't simply acquire technique — he learned how to translate the taste of one culture into the language of another. That foundation of classical French rigour and deep East Asian sensibility is what underpins everything SAN stands for today.

He returned to Korea and opened his first independent restaurant in 2024. SAN is not a showcase for what he learned abroad — it is closer to a personal reckoning: after years away, a careful re-examination of Korean flavour through his own eyes and hands.

Two currents run through the food simultaneously. The first is the precision absorbed from world-class kitchens — the calibration of sauce density and temperature, the contrast of textures, the deliberate arc of flavour within a single plate. The second is the Korea lodged in his memory: jeotgal, dongchimi, milmyeon, dwaeji gukbap. Both are present, and neither overwhelms the other.

The Michelin Guide describes SAN as a restaurant that pairs the intensity of seasonal ingredients with complex sauces while maintaining restrained balance. It singles out the abalone jang, tofu flower, and pork gukbap as signature expressions of Chef Cho's imagination and Korean interpretation. That direction was unmistakable in the summer course.

The meal opens with the coolness and bright acidity of summer produce, then builds gradually — through the umami of seafood, the depth of fermentation, and the warmth of meat and fire — without ever becoming heavy. The sauces are layered but never mask the ingredient. The technique is evident but never intrudes. You sense the years in America and Europe, but every dish ultimately resolves toward Korea.

The space suits the name perfectly. Tucked underground, the restaurant is insulated from the city above. Inside, the architecture echoes forests and ridgelines — tree-ring textures, restrained lighting, dark tones — creating a quiet that allows the food and wine to hold the room. The Michelin Guide cites the forest-like atmosphere, unobtrusive service, and thoughtful wine pairing as essential elements of the SAN experience.

In 2026, SAN received the Asia's 50 Best Restaurants One To Watch Award — recognition for a restaurant still early in its journey, yet already carrying the weight of everything Chef Cho accumulated across the world's great kitchens.

A chef who spent years in the world's finest kitchens, finally returning to the taste he was always moving toward. The summer at Restaurant SAN felt like both a record of the distance he has travelled and a clear signal of where he intends to go.
